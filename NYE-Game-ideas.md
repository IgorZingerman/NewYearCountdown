# NYE Games Ideas

## 1. The Blind Artist (Active Drawing Relay)

This game combines activity, communication, and hilarious results.

    Equipment: Large notepad/whiteboard, markers, and blindfolds.

    How to Play:

        Divide the group into teams (e.g., 4-6 people per team).

        The first player on the team is blindfolded and given the marker.

        A specific, simple object (e.g., a house, a car, a Christmas tree) is whispered to the blindfolded person's teammates, but not to the "artist."

        The teammates must shout instructions to the blindfolded artist on how to draw the object—but they cannot say the name of the object! (e.g., "Draw a triangle at the top!" "Now two squares underneath!")

        The first team to correctly guess what the blindfolded artist drew (or the one that finishes a recognizable picture first) wins.

    Age Range Tip: The 10-year-olds will love giving the frantic instructions, and the adults will love trying to decipher the abstract art they create!


## 2. Balloon Stomp

This is a fast-paced game that gets everyone moving and laughing.

    Equipment: Balloons and string/yarn.

    Preparation: Inflate a balloon for every player and tie it securely to a piece of string or yarn. Tie the other end of the string around the player's ankle, ensuring the balloon rests on the floor or near their foot.

    How to Play:

        Designate a play area.

        When the New Year's music starts, the objective is for each person to stomp on and pop the balloons of the other players while simultaneously protecting their own balloon.

        If your balloon is popped, you are out, and you stand on the sidelines to cheer.

        The last person (or a designated last few people) with an unpopped balloon is the winner.

    Age Range Tip: This is great physical fun for all ages. Just make sure the play area is clear of trip hazards!


## 3. Obstacle Course Relay (Office Supply Edition)

Use simple, common household items to create a hilarious, impromptu relay race. This is best played in teams.

    Equipment: Spoons, cotton balls, small bouncy balls, straws, tape, paper plates.

    Sample Challenges (The Relay Legs):

        The Cotton Ball Crawl: Players must move a cotton ball from one end of a table to the other using only a straw (blowing through it).

        The Spoon Race: Players balance a small bouncy ball or an egg on a spoon and walk a short distance to a checkpoint and back, passing it off to the next person. If the ball drops, they must restart the leg.

        The Tape Transfer: Two players in the relay must work together to move a piece of tape (sticky side out) from one person's forehead to the other's without using their hands.

    How to Play: The relay starts, and the first player on Team A runs to the start of the first challenge, completes it, and runs back to tag the next player, who does the second challenge, and so on. The first team to complete all challenges wins.

    Age Range Tip: Relays are fantastic for group dynamics. You can assign the younger guests easier legs and the adults the trickier ones, or mix the age groups within the teams for maximum fun and coaching.

## 4. The Story Chain (New Year's Edition)

This cooperative game is low-stakes and allows everyone, from the youngest to the oldest, to contribute to a funny, evolving story.

    Equipment: None (or a small object to pass, like a party hat).

    How to Play:

        The first person starts a story with a single sentence (e.g., "Last New Year's Eve, a gigantic, purple penguin waddled into the party.").

        The next person immediately adds another sentence to continue the plot, building on the previous line.

        Continue around the group until everyone has contributed at least one sentence (or two, or three, depending on the size of the group).

        The Goal: The game isn't competitive; the goal is simply to tell the most outrageous and entertaining collaborative story possible.

    Age Range Tip: The 10-year-olds can drive the story in hilarious, unexpected directions, and the adults will enjoy the challenge of logically connecting the plot points. You can even record the final story and read it back for extra laughs.

## 5. Ping-Pong Pass (The Team Roll)

This is played as a relay race, requiring players to use minimal movement to guide a small object.

    Equipment:

        One Ping-Pong Ball (or a small, light object like a cotton ball) per team.

        One Paper Towel Tube (or a piece of stiff cardboard rolled into a tube) per team.

    Setup:

        Divide guests into teams (4-6 people per team). Have each team line up facing the same direction.

        Give the first person in the line the ping-pong ball and the tube.

    How to Play:

        The goal is to pass the ping-pong ball from the first person in line to the last person in line without using hands and without dropping the ball.

        The first player places the tube on their shoulder, head, or a fixed spot on their body (e.g., the bridge of the nose, if they can balance it). They use the tube to nudge the ball forward, trying to get it onto the tube of the person in front of them.

        Players can only use the tube and their bodies (no hands!) to manipulate the ball.

        If the ball drops, the team must restart the pass from the beginning of the line.

        The first team to successfully pass the ball all the way to the last person in line wins.

    Age Range Tip: This is great for all ages. Younger children can focus on the careful receiving of the ball, while older guests will enjoy the challenge of balancing and nudging it. It’s a hilarious, low-impact exercise in communication and patience.

## 6. The Mummification Wrap Relay

    Equipment: Several rolls of toilet paper (the cheaper and thinner, the better) or party streamers.

    Setup: Divide the guests into teams of 3–4 people. Each team designates one person to be the "Mummy."

    How to Play:

        Place the "Mummy" in the center of their team.

        When the timer starts, the remaining team members race to completely wrap their Mummy in the toilet paper/streamer from head to toe (leaving a little space for the eyes and mouth).

        The wrappers must hold the paper—it cannot simply be placed on the floor to be rolled up.

        The goal is to use the entire roll and cover the Mummy as thoroughly as possible.

        The first team whose Mummy is fully wrapped (and the paper is torn or taped off) wins. Bonus points can be given for the neatest or most complete coverage!

    Age Range Tip: This is fantastic for mixed-age groups. The younger kids love the energy of the wrapping process and the silliness of being the Mummy, and the adults will focus on strategizing the fastest and most efficient wrapping method.
